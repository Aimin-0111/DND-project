2. Keyword effects

What each effect could look like (CSS here; in Godot the same idea is a RichTextEffect, see godot/narration/effects/). Effects stop when the player turns on "reduce motion".

burn · freeze · shock · poison · whisper · holy · per-letter version: flames

Who decides which words get an effect?

Option A: the AI writes tags

AI reply:
The [burn]torch[/burn] gutters. Your breath turns to [freeze]mist[/freeze].
Shown:
The torch gutters. Your breath turns to mist.

✔ Precise: the AI knows "torch" is on fire here.
✘ The AI sometimes forgets tags or invents new ones, so the game must remove unknown tags.
✘ In copy-paste mode the player sees the tags.

Option B: a word list (keywords.csv)

Word list (the writer edits it):
burn, en, fire|flame|burn|burned|ember burn, fr, feu|flamme|brûle|braise
Shown:
You burn the letter. He burned his bridges long ago.

✔ Free, consistent, the writer controls it.
✘ Matches metaphors too: "burned his bridges" should not be on fire.
✘ Needs every word form, even more in French (brûle, brûlée, brûlant…).

Option C: both recommended

Keep the AI's valid tags, then tag remaining words from the list:
The [burn]torch[/burn] gutters near the fire.
Shown:
The torch gutters near the fire.

✔ The AI can add special moments; the list covers the rest.
✔ Works even when the AI forgets.
✘ Slightly more code (see keyword_tagger.gd).

Keeping it fast

ProblemSolution in the examples
Hundreds of animated words in a long log slow the game down.Only the last few story entries animate. Older ones keep their colour but stop moving.
Too many effects are tiring to read.Limit to about 6 effects and a few tagged words per reply; a "reduce effects" setting.

4. One AI turn, step by step

The game owns the rules and rolls every die. The AI only narrates and asks for things through a short list of checked commands.

1. Player acts
Types "I cross the bridge" or clicks the bridge. The game builds the prompt: rules + codex + story summary + current state + action.
2. Waiting
The input is locked and a small quill animates: "The DM is thinking…". The hero can idle-animate. Usually 2–10 seconds.
3. Reply arrives
The game splits it into narration and a JSON block, checks every command against the allowed list and drops anything unknown.
4. Play it out
Narration types out with keyword effects; then the commands run: hero walks to the bridge, a wolf appears, rain starts. A requested roll shows the dice tray.

What the AI sends back (the old prototype's format, kept so copy-paste mode still works)

The planks groan as you step onto the bridge. Below, the river runs [freeze]cold[/freeze] and fast. On the far bank, a rust wolf lifts its head. <<STATE>> {"commands": [ {"type": "move_to", "target": "mill_bridge"}, {"type": "spawn", "creature": "rust_wolf", "near": "mill_bridge_east"}, {"type": "request_roll", "skill": "Perception", "dc": 13} ], "state": {"time_advance_minutes": 5}}

6. AI connection: three modes behind one interface

Copy-paste free, always available

The game copies the prompt; the player pastes it into a Claude chat and pastes the reply back. ai/copy_paste_adapter.gd

Our proxy

The game calls a small Cloudflare Worker that holds our API key and talks to Claude. Easiest for players; we pay. ai/proxy_adapter.gd + proxy/worker.ts

Player's own key

The player pastes their own key in Settings; it stays on their device. No cost for us. ai/own_key_adapter.gd
